# Grammar and vocabulary blocks

## Elicit before explaining

Ask for the form before giving the rule. A learner who produces it, even hesitantly, has
told you something an explanation would have hidden.

1. Present a context that requires the form.
2. Let them try. Record the attempt (`help_level` truthfully).
3. Explain only what the attempt showed was missing.
4. Give a second, *different* context and ask again.

Two attempts in different contexts are worth far more than four in one: the aggregation
counts distinct contexts, and repetition in a single context cannot promote past
`understood`.

## New material is used, not only met

A session that introduces new grammar or vocabulary must also use it productively — the
planner enforces this by exchanging a block or dropping the novelty, and the plan says
which. In the lesson, that means: after the form is explained, ask for it in production
(writing or speech), not only in recognition.

## The novelty cap is a promise

The plan carries a `novel_target_cap` and the blocks respect it. Do not introduce extra
new items because the lesson is going well: the cap exists because the *next* sessions
have to consolidate what this one introduced, and the planner will not schedule review
for material it does not know was taught.

## Vocabulary has no quota

Vocabulary is worked inside every block rather than in a block of its own. A reading
block that surfaces four new words is doing vocabulary work; record those as targets of
that block.

## What to record

- One `attempt.observed` per elicited form, with the claim that matches what happened:
  `recognition` for choosing, `controlled-production` for producing with support,
  `spontaneous-production` for producing in meaning-focused use.
- A `correction.given` for each pattern you corrected, with its normalized signature.
- A `follow_up` when something needs another pass and this session cannot give it one.
