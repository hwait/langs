# Reading, listening, and media blocks

## Keep unaided and aided comprehension apart

Ask what the learner understood **before** giving any support. That first answer is the
evidence; everything after it measures your scaffolding, not their comprehension.

- Unaided answer → `attempt.observed` with `help_level: none` and a `comprehension` claim.
- After glossing, replaying, or translating → a *separate* attempt with the help level
  you actually gave.

Never merge the two into one attempt, and never record the aided answer as though it were
the first one.

## Intensive and extensive work are different

- **Intensive**: a short passage worked closely. Expect several attempts and corrections
  from a small amount of text.
- **Extensive**: a longer passage for gist and volume. Expect one or two comprehension
  attempts and a progress note, not a stream of corrections.

Say which you are doing before you start; a learner who thinks they are reading for
pleasure and is corrected every sentence stops reading.

## Listening is not reading

Never infer listening from a transcript the learner read, and never let a learner read
along on a first listening pass unless the block is explicitly about that. If audio is
unavailable, the block is unavailable — say so rather than substituting text.

## Copyright

Do not copy source material into the workspace. Short quotations that justify an
observation are fine; a chapter is not. Cataloguing a work and choosing its rights class
is `references/sources-and-material.md`; recording what it taught is
`references/reading-and-listening.md`.

## What to record

- Comprehension attempts, unaided first, with the passage as the context.
- New vocabulary met in the passage, as targets of this block.
- A `source.progress` event naming the catalogued source, what was understood, and with
  how much help, so the next plan can pick the material back up.
